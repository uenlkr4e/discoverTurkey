Turkey is large, and its climate varies a lot between regions: the Mediterranean coast can be warm enough for swimming while snow covers central Anatolia. The best time to visit depends on where you're going and what you want to do.

## The short answer

For a classic first trip that combines Istanbul, Cappadocia and the coast, **late April to early June** and **September to October** are the best times. The weather is warm without the peak summer heat, the sea is swimmable on the south coast for much of that time, and sights are less crowded than in July and August.

## Season by season

### Spring (March to May)

Spring brings mild weather and green landscapes, and is ideal for sightseeing and hiking. Istanbul's tulip displays peak in April. The Aegean and Mediterranean seas are still cool until late May, and higher areas can be chilly at night.

### Summer (June to August)

Summer is hot and dry almost everywhere, very hot on the south coast and inland, and the peak season for beach holidays. It's the busiest and often most expensive time, especially during Turkish school holidays. Sightseeing at exposed ancient sites is tiring in the midday heat, so plan early starts. In recent summers, several major sites, including Ephesus, Hierapolis (Pamukkale), Patara and Side, have opened into the evening as part of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's [Night Museums](https://goturkiye.com/night-museums) programme.

### Autumn (September to November)

Early autumn is many travellers' favourite season: the sea is at its warmest, the light is softer, and crowds thin after mid-September. By November it is cooler and wetter, and some coastal businesses close for winter.

### Winter (December to February)

Winters are cool and rainy in Istanbul and on the coasts, and cold with snow in central and eastern Anatolia. It's a good time for Istanbul's museums and for snowy Cappadocia, with lower hotel demand. Many hotels and boat operators in small coastal resorts close until spring.

Closer to your trip, check the forecast with the [Turkish State Meteorological Service (MGM)](https://www.mgm.gov.tr/), which also issues official weather warnings.

## Holidays to plan around

Turkey has two major religious holidays, **Ramazan Bayramı** (at the end of Ramadan) and **Kurban Bayramı**. Their dates follow the Islamic lunar calendar and move about 11 days earlier each year. During these holidays many Turks travel, buses and flights fill up, and coastal resorts get busy, while some offices and shops close.

National holidays include New Year's Day, 23 April (National Sovereignty and Children's Day), 1 May, 19 May, 15 July, 30 August and 29 October (Republic Day). Check the exact dates for your year of travel and book transport early if your trip overlaps a holiday.
