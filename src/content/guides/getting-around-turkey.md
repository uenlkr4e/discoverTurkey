Turkey is a big country: driving from Istanbul to Antalya takes most of a day, and the far east is further still. Choosing the right mix of flights, buses, trains and ferries makes a big difference to a trip.

## Domestic flights

For long distances, flying is usually the fastest option and often good value if booked ahead. Istanbul's two airports, Istanbul Airport (IST) and Sabiha Gökçen (SAW), connect to dozens of cities, and Ankara and İzmir are secondary hubs. Useful routes for travellers include Istanbul to Kayseri or Nevşehir for Cappadocia, Istanbul to İzmir for Ephesus, and Istanbul to Antalya or Dalaman for the south coast.

- **Check which Istanbul airport you're using.** IST and SAW are on opposite sides of the city, and the transfer between them takes a long time.
- **Allow time for security.** Turkish airports often have a security check at the terminal entrance as well as before the gates.
- **Check the baggage allowance.** The cheapest fares on some airlines don't include checked bags.

## Long-distance buses

Turkey's intercity coach network is extensive, comfortable and reliable. Major companies run modern coaches with reserved seats, onboard drinks and regular rest stops. Overnight buses are common on long routes and save a night's accommodation.

- **Buying tickets.** Buy from company offices, at the bus station (*otogar*), or through the companies' apps and websites.
- **Getting to the centre.** Bus stations are often on the edge of town. Many companies run a free *servis* shuttle minibus to the centre; ask when you book.
- **Seating.** Seats are allocated, and unrelated men and women are not usually seated together.

## Trains

Turkey's high-speed trains (YHT) are fast and comfortable on the routes they serve, linking Istanbul, Ankara, Eskişehir and Konya. Conventional and regional trains are slower but scenic. In the Aegean, regional and suburban trains are a practical way to reach Selçuk for Ephesus.

Popular trains, including the long-distance sleeper routes, can sell out, so book as early as you can.

## Ferries and boats

In Istanbul, city ferries are cheap and frequent, and a beautiful way to cross between Europe and Asia. Ferries also cross the Sea of Marmara and link the Aegean coast with nearby Greek islands. In İzmir, ferries cross the bay. On the south coast, boats are more for excursions than transport.

## Driving

Hiring a car is the easiest way to explore Cappadocia, the Aegean countryside and the Turquoise Coast.

- **Rules of the road.** Traffic drives on the right. Seatbelts are compulsory, and police speed checks are common.
- **Tolls.** Motorways and some bridges are tolled electronically, and hire cars normally come with a toll tag. Ask the hire company how tolls are charged to you.
- **City driving.** Driving in Istanbul is stressful and parking is hard. Use public transport in the city and pick up a car only when you leave it.
- **Licence.** Carry your driving licence and passport. Check whether you also need an International Driving Permit for your nationality.

## Getting around cities

Most large cities have rechargeable transport cards that work on metros, trams, buses and ferries. *Dolmuş* minibuses run fixed routes and stop on request. They're cheap and useful in smaller towns and coastal areas. Taxis are widely available; in Istanbul, using a taxi app helps avoid misunderstandings over routes and fares.

Timetables, routes and fares change, especially seasonally. Check with the operator before you travel.
