Turkey's currency is the **Turkish lira** (TRY, written ₺ or TL). Prices have changed quickly in recent years, so this guide focuses on how to pay rather than on what things cost. Check current exchange rates before and during your trip, and expect prices to differ from those in older reviews and guidebooks.

## Cards

Debit and credit cards are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, shops, supermarkets and for most transport tickets, especially in cities and tourist areas. Contactless payment is common.

- **Pay in lira.** If a card machine or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency (dynamic currency conversion), decline. Paying in lira is almost always cheaper.
- **Tell your bank.** Let your bank know you are travelling, and check its foreign transaction fees.
- **Carry a backup.** Bring a second card from a different network in case one is declined.

## Cash

You'll still need some cash for small cafés, markets, *dolmuş* minibuses, tips, public toilets and village shops.

- **ATMs.** ATMs (*bankamatik*) are everywhere in towns and cities. Use machines attached to banks where possible, and check the fee shown on screen before confirming. Some charge a fixed fee per withdrawal, so fewer, larger withdrawals can work out cheaper.
- **Exchange.** Exchange offices (*döviz*) often give better rates than banks or airport counters. Compare the buying rate before you change money.
- **Small notes.** Keep some small notes and coins for minor purchases; large notes can be hard to change.

## Tipping

Tipping is customary but modest.

- **Restaurants.** Leave a small percentage in restaurants if service isn't included, more for excellent service. Rounding up is fine in cafés.
- **Hotels and guides.** Small tips for hotel porters and housekeeping are appreciated. For tours, tip the guide and driver separately if you were happy with the service.
- **Taxis.** Rounding up to a convenient amount is usual.

## Bargaining

Haggling is expected in bazaars and souvenir shops, but not in supermarkets, chain stores, restaurants or anywhere with clearly marked fixed prices.

- **Before you start.** Have a price in mind, and know when you're happy to walk away.
- **Keep it friendly.** Polite, good-humoured negotiation goes further than hard bargaining.
- **Only make offers you mean.** Once you've agreed a price, it's considered rude to walk away.
- **Expensive items.** For carpets and jewellery, take your time, compare shops, and ask for a written receipt and certificate of origin.

## Budgeting

Turkey can still be good value, but costs vary widely between regions and seasons. Istanbul and the resort areas in summer are the most expensive. Check recent prices for your hotel, tours and transport when you book, and allow a margin for change.
