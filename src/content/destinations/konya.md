Konya sits on the high Anatolian plain, in a region that has been settled since prehistoric times. In the 12th and 13th centuries it was the capital of the **Seljuk Sultanate of Rum**, and its finest monuments, carved stone gateways, tiled domes and madrasas, date from that golden age. It's best known as the home of **Rumi**, the poet and mystic whose followers founded the Mevlevi order, the whirling dervishes.

Today Konya is a large, modern and fairly conservative city. Few international tourists stay long, which makes it a rewarding, unhurried stop between Cappadocia and the coast.

## Seeing the whirling dervishes

The *sema*, the whirling ceremony of the Mevlevi dervishes, is a religious ritual, not a show. It is listed by UNESCO as part of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Watch quietly, don't applaud, and don't use flash photography.

The most important time to see it is **Şeb-i Arus**, held every year from 7 to 17 December to mark the anniversary of Rumi's death in 1273. Tickets for the ceremonies sell out, and hotels fill up, so book well ahead. Outside December, ceremonies are held more occasionally. Check the current programme with the Mevlana Cultural Centre or the official [GoTürkiye](https://goturkiye.com/konya) site.

## Visiting respectfully

The Mevlana Museum and the city's mosques are active places of worship and pilgrimage. Wear clothes that cover your shoulders and knees, and women should bring a scarf. Expect mosques to close to visitors briefly during prayer times. Opening hours and ticket arrangements for the state museums are listed on the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's [official museum site](https://muze.gov.tr/).

## How long to stay

One full day covers the Mevlana Museum, Alaeddin Hill and the madrasa museums. A second day lets you visit Çatalhöyük and Sille, or see a *sema* ceremony in the evening.
