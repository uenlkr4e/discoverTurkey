attractions:
  - name: Hagia Sophia
    description: Built as a cathedral in the sixth century, later a mosque, then a museum, and a mosque again since 2020. Its vast dome and Byzantine mosaics make it the city's essential sight. It's a working mosque, so dress modestly and expect visits to pause around prayer times.
  - name: Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque)
    description: Facing Hagia Sophia across Sultanahmet Square, this early 17th-century mosque takes its nickname from the blue İznik tiles inside. Visitors use a separate entrance, and the interior closes to visitors during prayers.
  - name: Topkapı Palace
    description: The Ottoman sultans' palace for nearly four centuries, set around a series of courtyards above the meeting of the Bosphorus and the Golden Horn. Allow at least half a day, including the Harem, which is usually ticketed separately.
  - name: Basilica Cistern
    description: A sixth-century underground reservoir held up by hundreds of columns, two of which rest on carved Medusa heads. Cool, atmospheric and a good escape from summer heat.
  - name: Grand Bazaar and Spice Bazaar
    description: The Grand Bazaar is one of the oldest and largest covered markets in the world, a maze of streets selling carpets, lamps, ceramics and jewellery. The smaller Spice Bazaar near Eminönü is best for Turkish delight, dried fruit and spices.
  - name: Süleymaniye Mosque
    description: Mimar Sinan's masterpiece crowns the city's third hill. It's calmer than Sultanahmet, and its terraces have some of the best views over the Golden Horn.
  - name: A Bosphorus ferry
    description: Public ferries between Europe and Asia are one of the city's great pleasures, and part of everyday life. Even a short crossing from Eminönü or Karaköy to Kadıköy or Üsküdar shows the skyline at its best.
gettingThere: |
  Most international flights arrive at **Istanbul Airport (IST)** on the European side, about 40 km north of the old city. Some airlines use **Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW)** on the Asian side. Both are linked to the city by airport buses, metro lines and taxis. Check the current routes on the airport websites before you fly.

  Istanbul is also the hub of Turkey's long-distance bus network, and high-speed trains link it with Ankara and Konya.
gettingAround: |
  Buy an **Istanbulkart**, the rechargeable card used on the metro, trams, buses, Marmaray rail tunnel and city ferries. The T1 tram runs through the historic peninsula, linking Sultanahmet, Eminönü and Kabataş, and the Marmaray line crosses under the Bosphorus.

  The historic core is walkable, but hilly. Taxis are plentiful; using a ride-hailing or taxi app makes fares and routes clearer.
foodAndExperiences: |
  Start the day with a Turkish breakfast (*kahvaltı*) of cheese, olives, tomatoes, eggs and fresh bread, or grab a sesame *simit* from a street cart. Try fish sandwiches by the water at Eminönü, *meze* and grilled fish in a *meyhane*, and baklava with a glass of tea or Turkish coffee.

  For a slower evening, cross to Kadıköy on the Asian side for its food market streets, or watch the sunset over the Golden Horn from the hills of Süleymaniye or Galata.

Istanbul has been an imperial capital for more than 1,500 years: Byzantium, then Constantinople, the heart of both the Byzantine and Ottoman empires. Its skyline of domes and minarets rises from a peninsula between the Golden Horn and the Sea of Marmara, while the Bosphorus strait divides the city between Europe and Asia.

Most first-time visitors base themselves in or near **Sultanahmet**, the historic core, where Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace and the Basilica Cistern are all within a few minutes' walk. Neighbourhoods across the Golden Horn, such as **Karaköy** and **Galata**, have a livelier café and gallery scene and are well connected by tram and ferry.

## Planning your days

With three days you can cover the main sights of the historic peninsula, the bazaars and a ferry trip. A fourth day lets you slow down: explore the colourful streets of Balat and Fener, visit Dolmabahçe Palace on the Bosphorus, or spend an afternoon in Kadıköy on the Asian shore.

The big sights get very busy, especially from late morning in summer and around cruise-ship arrivals. Visit the most popular ones as soon as they open. Remember that the mosques close to visitors during prayer times, including the longer Friday midday prayer.

## Neighbourhoods to know

- **Sultanahmet**: the historic heart, with the major monuments. Convenient, but quiet in the evening.
- **Sirkeci and Eminönü**: transport hubs by the ferry piers and the Spice Bazaar.
- **Galata, Karaköy and Beyoğlu**: hillside streets, cafés, galleries and İstiklal Avenue.
- **Kadıköy and Moda**: the Asian side's lively, local-feeling food and nightlife district.
- **Balat and Fener**: historic Greek, Jewish and Armenian quarters along the Golden Horn, known for their colourful houses.

## Tips for a smoother visit

Carry a scarf, and wear clothes that cover your shoulders and knees, for visiting mosques. Keep small change for ferries, tea and street food. Check opening days and ticket arrangements on the official sites before you go: hours and prices for the major museums change from time to time.

Tickets for state museums such as Topkapı Palace and the Istanbul Archaeology Museums are sold online on the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's [official museum site](https://muze.gov.tr/), which also sells the **MuseumPass İstanbul**. If you plan to see several museums, compare its cost with individual tickets. For more ideas and city events, see the official [GoTürkiye Istanbul pages](https://goturkiye.com/istanbul).
