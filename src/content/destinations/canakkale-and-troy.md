gettingThere: |
  Buses run frequently from Istanbul to Çanakkale. The journey takes around five to six hours and crosses the strait by bridge or ferry. **Çanakkale Airport (ÇKZ)** has a small number of flights. Buses also link Çanakkale with İzmir and the Aegean coast.

  Coming from Istanbul by car, the **1915 Çanakkale Bridge** crosses the Dardanelles between Gelibolu and Lapseki. It's tolled.
gettingAround: |
  Car ferries cross the Dardanelles frequently between Çanakkale and Eceabat or Kilitbahir, on the Gallipoli peninsula. Minibuses run from Çanakkale to Troy, about 30 km south.

  The Gallipoli battlefields are spread out, so most visitors join a guided tour or hire a car. A knowledgeable guide makes a great difference to understanding the campaign.

Çanakkale is a relaxed university town on the Asian shore of the **Dardanelles**, the narrow strait that links the Sea of Marmara with the Aegean. For millennia this crossing between Europe and Asia has been fought over, and two of the world's most famous conflicts took place within sight of it: the legendary Trojan War and the Gallipoli Campaign of 1915.

Çanakkale makes a comfortable base, with plenty of hotels and restaurants along the seafront and easy ferries across the strait.

## Visiting Troy

Troy can be underwhelming if you expect grand monuments: the remains are mostly walls, ramps and foundations from many different periods. Its importance lies in the layers, and in the story of how Heinrich Schliemann's 19th-century excavations, and more careful archaeology since, uncovered them. A guide, or a visit to the **Troy Museum** first, brings it to life.

Tickets for both are sold on the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's [official museum site](https://muze.gov.tr/). Allow two to three hours for the site and at least an hour for the museum.

## The Gallipoli battlefields

The **Gallipoli Campaign** of 1915–16 cost the lives of well over 100,000 soldiers. For Turks, it's remembered as the *Çanakkale Savaşı*, a defining moment in the country's history and in the career of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. For Australians and New Zealanders, it's the origin of Anzac Day. The peninsula is a protected historical site, and many visitors come as a pilgrimage.

Two dates bring large crowds:

- **18 March**, Çanakkale Victory and Martyrs' Day, a major Turkish commemoration.
- **25 April**, Anzac Day, with a dawn service at Anzac Cove. If you plan to attend, check the arrangements published by the Australian and New Zealand governments well in advance.

Wear sturdy shoes, bring water, and remember that these are cemeteries and memorials: visit quietly and respectfully.

## Beyond Çanakkale

With an extra day, head south to **Assos** for its temple and harbour, or take the ferry to **Bozcaada** for wine, beaches and a slower pace. For more ideas, see the official [GoTürkiye Çanakkale pages](https://goturkiye.com/canakkale).
