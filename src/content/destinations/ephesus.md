gettingThere: |
  The base for Ephesus is **Selçuk**, about 3 km from the site. From İzmir, trains on the İZBAN suburban line and regional trains run to Selçuk, and buses run from İzmir's bus station. **İzmir Airport (ADB)** is about an hour's drive away.

  Cruise passengers arrive at **Kuşadası**, about 20 km away. If you're not on a cruise, check which days ships are in port and avoid them if you can.
gettingAround: |
  From Selçuk, take a taxi or local minibus to the site, or walk along the road (about 3 km). The site has two entrances: starting at the **upper (south) gate** and walking downhill to the **lower gate** saves a climb in the heat. Taxis and minibuses can drop you at one gate and collect you from the other.

  Selçuk itself is small and walkable, and also makes a good base for Şirince and the beaches near Kuşadası.

Ephesus was one of the largest and wealthiest cities of the Roman Empire, the capital of the province of Asia and a major port. Its harbour has long since silted up, leaving the ruins several kilometres inland, but the extent of what survives is extraordinary: paved streets, temples, baths, fountains, the great theatre and the reconstructed façade of the Library of Celsus. Ephesus has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2015.

## Planning your visit

Allow at least three hours for the main site, more if you visit the Terrace Houses. The ruins are spread out, exposed and largely unshaded, and the marble paving can be slippery and uneven.

- **Arrive early.** The site is busiest from mid-morning, especially when cruise ships are in port.
- **Walk downhill.** Start at the upper gate and finish at the lower gate near the theatre.
- **Bring water, a hat and sunscreen.** There's little shade, even in spring.
- **Consider a guide or audio guide.** The ruins make far more sense with context. Licensed guides can be hired at the entrances; agree the length and price beforehand.

## Why stay in Selçuk

Many visitors see Ephesus on a day trip from İzmir, Kuşadası or further afield. Staying a night or two in **Selçuk** lets you visit at opening time before the tour groups. It also leaves time for the town's own sights: the Ephesus Museum, the Basilica of St John, the İsa Bey Mosque and the Temple of Artemis site, all within walking distance of each other.

## Accessibility

Parts of the main route are walkable for people with limited mobility, especially between the lower gate and the theatre, but expect steps, slopes and uneven stone throughout. Ask at the ticket office about current access arrangements.
