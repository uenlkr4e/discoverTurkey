Pamukkale, "cotton castle" in Turkish, is a hillside of dazzling white travertine terraces in the Lycus valley of south-western Turkey. Hot springs rich in calcium carbonate have flowed down the slope for thousands of years, leaving layer upon layer of white mineral deposits and shallow, turquoise pools. On the plateau above lie the ruins of **Hierapolis**, a Roman spa city that grew up around the same springs. Together they have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988.

The terraces have been carefully protected since hotels on the plateau were removed in the late 20th century. Today you visit along set paths, and the flow of water is managed so the white surface can regenerate.

## Planning your visit

One ticket covers the travertines, Hierapolis and the archaeology museum. The Antique Pool is extra. Tickets can be bought on the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's [official museum site](https://muze.gov.tr/), and the site is included in the **MuseumPass The Aegean**. Check current opening hours before you go. In recent summers, Hierapolis has also opened into the evening as part of the [Night Museums](https://goturkiye.com/night-museums) programme.

- **Allow most of a day.** Hierapolis is much larger than many people expect, and is worth at least three hours on top of the terraces.
- **Bring a bag for your shoes.** You'll carry them across the travertines. The wet surface can be slippery, and it's rough in places.
- **Protect yourself from the sun.** The white rock reflects heat and glare, and there's little shade.
- **Bring swimwear** if you want to use the Antique Pool.

## Getting the most from a short visit

Day trips from the coast arrive in the late morning and leave by mid-afternoon. If you stay in Pamukkale village, you can be on the terraces at opening time, rest in the heat of the day and return for sunset. Combine the visit with Ephesus and Aphrodisias for a classic route through the ancient cities of western Turkey.
