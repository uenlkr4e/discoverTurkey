Gaziantep, often called simply **Antep**, is one of the largest cities in south-eastern Turkey and, for many Turks, the country's food capital. It's an energetic, prosperous place, with a compact old city of bazaars, workshops and stone mansions around its castle mound, and a modern city of parks and wide avenues around it.

Two things bring most visitors: the **food**, especially the baklava and kebabs, and the extraordinary Roman mosaics in the **Zeugma Mosaic Museum**.

## Before you go

Gaziantep is in a region close to the border with Syria. **Read your own government's travel advice for Turkey** before you plan a trip. Some governments advise against travel to areas near the border, and this may affect your travel insurance.

The province was among those affected by the **February 2023 earthquakes**. The city centre is open to visitors, and many historic buildings have been restored, but a few sites have reopened only recently or are still closed. Check current information for the places you most want to see.

## Zeugma and the mosaics

Zeugma was a wealthy Roman city on the Euphrates whose villas were decorated with superb floor mosaics. When a dam was built downstream around 2000, rising water threatened the site, and archaeologists raced to rescue as much as they could. Those mosaics, with others from the region, now fill the purpose-built Zeugma Mosaic Museum. Allow at least two hours.

Tickets are sold on the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's [official museum site](https://muze.gov.tr/).

## Eating well

Many of Gaziantep's best-known restaurants and baklava shops open early and close by mid-afternoon or early evening, once the day's food is sold. Eat your main meal at lunchtime, and ask locals for their favourite places. For more ideas, see the official [GoTürkiye gastronomy pages](https://goturkiye.com/gastronomy).
