The Turquoise Coast is the stretch of Turkey's south-western shore between the Gulf of Fethiye and the Antalya region. Pine-covered mountains drop straight into clear, blue-green water, and the coastline is broken up into coves, islands and lagoons. In antiquity this was **Lycia**, and its distinctive rock-cut tombs, carved like temple fronts into the cliffs, appear above harbours and roadsides all along the coast.

## Choosing a base

The coast is long, and the roads are winding, so most travellers pick one or two bases:

- **Fethiye**: a working town with a large harbour, good transport and markets, close to Ölüdeniz, Saklıkent and the boat trips to the Twelve Islands.
- **Ölüdeniz**: beach-focused and busy in summer, best for paragliding and the Blue Lagoon.
- **Kalkan**: a hillside village of whitewashed houses and rooftop restaurants, popular with villa holidays.
- **Kaş**: a lively small town with a harbour, a Hellenistic theatre and an outdoorsy crowd. It's the best base for Kekova, diving and the eastern sites.

Splitting a week between Fethiye and Kaş covers the highlights without too much driving.

## Boats and the Blue Voyage

A day on a boat is almost obligatory. Excursion boats leave each morning from Fethiye, Ölüdeniz and Kaş, stopping at bays for swimming. For a longer trip, *gulet* cruises (the "Blue Voyage") spend several nights sailing between coves. Choose operators with good recent reviews, and check what is included.

## Responsible travel

Patara and other beaches are important nesting sites for endangered loggerhead turtles. Follow the beach closing times and signs, don't leave umbrellas or rubbish on the sand, and keep lights away from the beach at night.
