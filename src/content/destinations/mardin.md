Mardin rises in tiers of golden limestone up a steep hillside, beneath a citadel and looking out over the vast, flat plain of **Upper Mesopotamia** towards Syria. For centuries it was a meeting point of cultures, with Muslim and Christian communities, Arabic, Kurdish, Syriac and Turkish speakers living side by side. Its mosques, madrasas, churches and grand stone houses make it one of the most beautiful old towns in Turkey.

Many of the old town's mansions have been restored as boutique hotels and restaurants, with rooftop terraces that are perfect for watching the sun set over the plain.

## Before you go

Mardin is close to the border with Syria. **Read your own government's travel advice for Turkey** before you plan a trip. Some governments advise against travel to areas near the border, and this may affect your travel insurance.

## Planning your days

- **Day one:** Explore the old town on foot, visiting the Great Mosque, the Zinciriye and Kasımiye madrasas and the Mardin Museum. End with sunset on a rooftop terrace.
- **Day two:** Visit Deyrulzafaran Monastery in the morning, then Dara, returning to town for dinner.
- **Day three:** Drive to Midyat and Mor Gabriel.

## Visiting monasteries and mosques

The monasteries around Mardin and Midyat are active religious communities. Dress modestly, keep your voice down, and ask before photographing people. Visiting hours can be limited, so check locally before you set off. In mosques, the usual etiquette applies: cover your shoulders and knees, and women should bring a scarf.
