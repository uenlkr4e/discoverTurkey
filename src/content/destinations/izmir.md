  **İzmir Adnan Menderes Airport (ADB)** has domestic flights from Istanbul, Ankara and other cities, and seasonal international routes. The İZBAN commuter train and buses link the airport with the city centre.

  Intercity buses arrive at the main bus station (*otogar*) on the city's eastern edge. Regional trains also serve İzmir from inland Aegean towns.
gettingAround: |
  İzmir has a metro, trams, the İZBAN suburban railway and city ferries across the bay, all paid with a rechargeable transport card. The ferry between Konak, Alsancak, Karşıyaka and other piers is cheap, quick and scenic.

  For day trips, İZBAN trains run south towards Selçuk for Ephesus. Buses and organised tours cover Pergamon, Şirince and the Çeşme peninsula.

İzmir, the ancient city of Smyrna, is Turkey's third-largest city and its main port on the Aegean. Rebuilt after a devastating fire in 1922, it is a modern, outward-looking city that many Turks consider the country's most relaxed. Life revolves around the long bay, the seafront Kordon and the old market streets of Kemeraltı.

Most travellers use İzmir as a base for the ancient sites and coastal towns of the Aegean, and it works very well for that. It's worth giving the city itself a day or two as well: it's a good place to see everyday Turkish urban life away from the main tourist circuit.

## Where to stay

**Alsancak**, behind the northern Kordon, has the most restaurants, cafés and bars, and is walkable to the seafront. **Konak** is convenient for the bazaar and transport connections. If you plan several day trips by train, staying near an İZBAN station saves time.

## Day trips from İzmir

- **Ephesus and Selçuk**: the great Roman city, the Temple of Artemis site and the Basilica of St John, south of the city and reachable by train.
- **Pergamon (Bergama)**: a UNESCO-listed Hellenistic acropolis with one of the steepest ancient theatres anywhere, and the Asklepion healing sanctuary.
- **Şirince**: a hillside village of restored stone houses near Selçuk, known for its fruit wines.
- **Çeşme and Alaçatı**: the peninsula's beaches and windsurfing, and Alaçatı's stone streets, busy with visitors in summer.

## Getting your bearings

The city wraps around the inner Gulf of İzmir. The historic centre and Alsancak are on the southern and eastern shore, and Karşıyaka on the northern shore. Ferries make crossing the bay part of the experience.
