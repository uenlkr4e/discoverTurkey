South-eastern Turkey rewards travellers with some of the country's best food, oldest archaeological sites and most striking old towns, far from the busy coastal resorts. This six-day road trip runs from Gaziantep, the food capital, along the Euphrates and through Şanlıurfa to the honey-coloured hill town of Mardin. It follows part of a culinary route promoted by [GoTürkiye](https://goturkiye.com/gastronomy), the official tourism site.

## Before you go

- **Check travel advice.** This route runs close to the border with Syria. Read your own government's travel advice for Turkey before you book, as it may affect your travel insurance.
- **Hire a car, or book a driver.** Public buses link the cities, but a car makes the day trips to Halfeti, Göbeklitepe, Deyrulzafaran and Midyat far easier. Fly into Gaziantep and out of Mardin to avoid backtracking.
- **Avoid midsummer.** July and August are extremely hot across the region. Spring and autumn are much more comfortable.
- **Dress modestly.** The southeast is more conservative than the coast. Cover your shoulders and knees, especially at mosques, shrines and monasteries.

## Adapting the plan

With more time, spend a night in **Şanlıurfa** to explore its bazaars and the Şanlıurfa Archaeology Museum, home to some of the oldest known statues in the world. You could also add **Diyarbakır**, with its great basalt city walls along the Tigris, between Mardin and your flight home.
